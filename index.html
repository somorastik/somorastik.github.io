How LubkaM1 is built

What this is

LubkaM1 is a study remake of a six-voice digital drum synthesizer. It loads kit (.SND) and pattern (.PAT) files and plays them through a sound engine ported line by line from open-source drum-synth firmware.

The engine runs in an AudioWorklet at 44.1 kHz. As in the firmware, envelopes, LFOs and modulation update once per 64-sample block (about 1.45 ms) and the audio path works in a signed 16-bit scale with saturating adds.

Drum voice signal path

Mod osc→FM or mix→Main osc+Click→ZDF filter→Amp EG × velocity→Drive→Volume, fader→Sample-rate reducer→Pan / out / FX bus

A decay envelope sweeps pitch and FM depth. One quirk is kept on purpose: the hardware converts the FM modulator with an unsigned cast, so only its positive half moves the carrier phase.

  • Drum 1–3: two oscillators (sine, triangle, saw, square, noise, pulse), pitch envelope, FM or mix mode.
  • Snare: tuned oscillator plus filtered noise with a mix control and a repeat envelope for claps.
  • Cymbal, hats: three-oscillator FM; the two hat tracks share one voice with closed and open decays, so they choke each other.
  • FX bus: tube, wavefolder or diode-clipper distortion, ring modulator, compressor, mono or ping-pong delay.

File formats decoded

  • Kit: 8-byte name, then 247 parameter bytes (0–127) in the firmware's parameter order.
  • Pattern: name, PatV03 tag, then for 7 tracks × 64 steps: velocity, probability, note and two automation slots; a flam/shift byte per step; four 16-bit step masks per track; shuffle, track lengths and the linked kit slot.
  • Firmware image: 16-byte header (8-byte tag, payload size, checksum) before raw ARM code. Versions 1.70 (275,832 bytes) and 1.71 (273,528 bytes) were inspected; the remake ports the published source rather than the compiled binary.

Sequencer

A 96 PPQ clock gives 24 ticks per step. Flams (×2 to ×8) and micro-shifts of ±7 × 3 ticks are scheduled from the previous, current and next step, as in the firmware. Swing delays every second step by up to two thirds of a step. Each step can automate two parameters, which stay set until that track plays its next step.

Not included

MIDI, song chaining, the Euclidean generator, sample upload and SD storage. The second stereo output and the single outputs are mixed into the browser's stereo output.

Using it

Drag a knob up or down (Shift for fine steps), scroll on it, or use the arrow keys; double-click returns it to the kit's stored value. Space starts and stops; keys 1–7 play the voices. Right-click, long-press or Shift-click a step to edit its velocity, probability, flam, shift and automation.
